## Facts (with sources)
- April 1975, a bar in Bonny Doon, California: ad man Gary Dahl listened to friends complain about their pets and
  joked that the perfect pet was a rock; he then wrote a training manual and sold it with a rock.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Rock
  - https://www.cbc.ca/archives/how-a-discussion-about-pets-turned-into-the-pet-rock-phenomenon-1.4893048
- The rocks were smooth stones from Rosarito Beach, Mexico, costing about a penny each.
  - https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/595180/pet-rock-history
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Rock
- Packaged in a cardboard "pet carrier" box with straw bedding and breathing holes, plus a manual,
  "The Care and Training of Your Pet Rock"; "sit" and "stay" were easy, "roll over" needed help from the trainer.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Rock
- Price $3.95. More than a million sold by Christmas 1975 (about 1.5 million in all); Dahl became a millionaire.
  - https://www.museumofplay.org/ (Pet Rock entry)
  - https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/business-suckers/26046
- His company was called Rock Bottom Productions.
  - https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/595180/pet-rock-history
- By February 1976 the rocks were being discounted; the fad lasted about six months.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Rock
- Depiction: Dahl is not named and is drawn as a generic 1970s ad man (no likeness). No brand logos on screen.

## Script (170 counted words)
1. In nineteen seventy-five, a man got rich selling rocks. As pets.
2. It started in a California bar. His friends were complaining about their pets. The feeding. The mess.
3. So he joked that the perfect pet was a rock.
4. Then he actually sold one.
5. He bought smooth stones from a Mexican beach, for a penny each.
6. Each one came in a cardboard pet carrier, with straw, and air holes to breathe.
7. Plus a training manual. "Sit" and "stay" were easy. "Roll over" needed a little help.
8. Price: three ninety-five.
9. By Christmas, over a million had sold. He named his company Rock Bottom Productions.
10. Luckily, it made him a millionaire.
11. Unluckily, by February, his pets were on sale.
12. A million people paid for a friend who can't do anything.
13. Because sometimes, you just want something that's yours.
14. A rock will never leave you. It will never say good morning, either.
15. That's why we made Feeloy. Jenny listens, remembers your day, and actually talks back.
16. Search Feeloy on the App Store.

## Shot plan (draft, timed to the take later)
1. Hook: a 1970s living room; a smooth grey rock sits on a velvet cushion like a pet; a man in a wide-collar suit pats it. Flash on "As pets."
2. A wood-panelled California bar at golden hour: the man on a stool, two friends grumbling; bubbles: a dog bowl, a muddy pawprint.
3. His face lights up: a lightbulb pop over a rock.
4. Cut: a little workshop table, a stamp, a box.
5. A Mexican beach: a bucket of smooth stones, a "1¢" coin pop.
6. Insert: the cardboard carrier box opens: straw nest, air holes, the rock inside (rock card with two little ink-dot "eyes"? no: a plain rock, the joke is that it's just a rock).
7. The manual pages: "SIT" (rock sits), "STAY" (rock stays), "ROLL OVER" (a finger nudges it over).
8. Price tag "$3.95" swings in.
9. A 1970s store window at night with snow and lights, stacks of carriers; a rolling counter to 1,000,000. Office door sign "ROCK BOTTOM PRODUCTIONS".
10. The man counting money, grin.
11. Deadpan: the same store, a "SALE" sign, the rocks in a bargain bin.
12-13. A lonely person in a small flat talks to a rock on the table; the rock says nothing.
14. Close on the rock; the clock ticks; nothing.
15. Phone insert: Jenny waves, a reply bubble pops, "good morning" sun icon.
16. End card.
