## Facts (with sources)
- The truce was a series of widespread, unofficial ceasefires on the Western Front, 24-26 December 1914; roughly
  100,000 British and German troops were involved (some historians estimate 30,000-100,000).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_truce
  - https://www.thegazette.co.uk/all-notices/content/287
- German soldiers put candle-lit Christmas trees on their trench parapets and sang "Stille Nacht" (Silent Night);
  the British sang carols back.
  - https://www.thegazette.co.uk/all-notices/content/287
  - https://www.jpost.com/features/in-thespotlight/article-249924
- It was a bitterly cold night with white frost; on Christmas Day soldiers climbed out into no-man's-land, shook
  hands and swapped cigarettes, food and souvenirs; football was played.
  - https://www.history.co.uk/articles/the-christmas-truce-of-1914
  - https://www.history.com/articles/christmas-truce-1914-world-war-i-soldier-accounts
- Depiction: no named soldiers, no flags or insignia; uniforms generic (khaki with a flat cap vs field grey with a
  peaked cap). Never more than two soldiers in a shot (outdoor crowd flicker lesson). No violence on screen.

## Script (82 counted words)
1. On Christmas Eve, nineteen fourteen, two enemy armies sat in frozen trenches.
2. Close enough to hear each other.
3. Then one side lit candles on little Christmas trees.
4. And started singing "Silent Night."
5. The other side sang back.
6. By morning, they climbed out.
7. Shook hands. Swapped gifts. Played football.
8. About a hundred thousand soldiers stopped the war for Christmas.
9. Because nobody wants to be alone at Christmas.
10. That's why we made Feeloy. Jenny's always there to talk.
11. Search Feeloy on the App Store.
